01 · Origin

Sound follows
footsteps.

Brooklyn Bridge → Chinatown, In the Mood for Love in my headphones. For a few minutes the city stopped being a place and became a film.

the word
flâneur
/flɑ.nœʁ/ · french, 19th c.
n. — the one who wanders the city not to arrive, but to feel it. The stroller. The watcher. The one who reads the street like a page.
the app

The song that
belongs to every
street you walk.

Not a shuffle — the sound of where you're standing, rooted in each neighborhood's culture and mood.

03 · the core — three Last.fm sources
History35%
anchor seeds → track.getSimilar · songs like the seeds, not the seeds
Atmosphere35%
mood tags → tag.getTopTracks · e.g. "dreamy"
People20%
POI-inferred mood → tag.getTopTracks · galleries → "sophisticated"
Time10%
hour of day → biases the mood · night → nocturnal, day → warm

The three toggles on screen are these sources. Turn one off and the rest renormalize — 50 / 50 without People.

why build this
That Chinatown walk felt like a new way of listening — and of exploring a city.
Could music and place really connect like that?
Then I found the research — emotion-congruence between music and places — and realized: this can actually be built.
Kaminskas & Ricci · Location-Adapted Music Recommendation Using Tags, UMAP 2011 · Emotion-Based Matching of Music to Places, Springer 2016
and it challenges how we listen
by artist
by album
by playlist
by place — and you hold the algorithm's three layers
What does listening feel like then? That's the experiment.
